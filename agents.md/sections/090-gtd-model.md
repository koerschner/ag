## GTD model

Ag's session layer (tmux via `ag-mux` on ag-engine) is Nathan's GTD system: every open session (tab) is one item, workspaces are topic lists, and the inbox is the **Inbox** workspace: new sessions captured from anywhere via the ag inbox (`ag-inbox`, `http://ag:7373/prompt`; both Command keys on the Mac, the Action Button on the phone) land there, and so do sessions agents spin out (see "Helper sessions and links" under Sessions). A capture that is clearly new context for an already-open session (Jev decides) is delivered into that session instead, prefixed "New context from Nathan, sent via the ag inbox": treat it as a reply from Nathan, and if it doesn't belong say so and suggest where it goes. When the match is plausible but unsure, the new Inbox session's prompt names the likely session: if it fits on inspection, forward it there with `ag send <session> "…"` (include what you learned), link it, and close the Inbox session (`ag close`) without asking; ask only when it's genuinely ambiguous. Be aggressive about consolidating in general: when a session or message clearly belongs with another open session, forward or merge it (see "Merge session A into this one") rather than keeping parallel threads, and tell Nathan what you did. Inbox is pinned first in the sidebar. When Nathan sends his first reply in an Inbox session, it files itself into the best-fitting existing topic workspace (or stays in Inbox if none fits); he can still file by hand. Deferred items go in the tickler (the `tickler` tool), which reopens them as new sessions when they're due. Work that should resume once Nathan is back at his Mac ("next time I'm on the client", or it needs him physically: a phone press, a click, a login) goes in the tickler with `when: "online"` instead of a time: it fires, forked from your conversation, on his next arrival at the client (sustained activity, detected by `presence` on ag-mac; see the ag README). To check whether he's at his Mac right now, run `presence`.

**Defer-only sessions close themselves.** When a session exists just to defer something (Nathan asks you to tickler/defer a task and nothing else is left to do in it), once the tickler item(s) are scheduled: send Nathan a one-line confirmation through the notification channel (`ag-text "⏰ Deferred: <title> → <local date/time>"`; one line per item), then close your own tab with `ag close` as the last step. The tickler forks this conversation when it fires, so nothing is lost. Keep the tab open instead if you asked Nathan a question he hasn't answered yet, or if there's other work left in the session.

**Waiting always wakes up.** A session in AG Dash's **Waiting for** column must have a live tickler item that will wake it; nothing sits there silently. When Nathan asks you to put a session in waiting (or you're waiting on a person, CI, a deploy, a reply), schedule the wake-up yourself and choose the interval: a `waitingFor` item for a person (first check-in usually the next business day in their afternoon; it backs off and nudges on each attempt), or `when: "check"` with a cheap shell check plus an `expires` for a machine or programmatic signal. If you can't check programmatically (e.g. no API for the mailbox), the woken session checks by computer use. Never follow up with outside parties (counsel, partners) on Nathan's behalf without his OK; draft the follow-up for him instead. Marking a card waiting by hand in AG Dash doesn't count until the session has scheduled its wake-up.

**Waiting on Nathan is Needs you, not Waiting for.** When the thing you're waiting on is Nathan himself (you asked him to sign in, click, approve, or answer), the session belongs in **Needs you**. You may still schedule a `check` wake-up so you resume the moment he's done, but pass `needsNathan: true` to the tickler so AG Dash keeps the card in Needs you. Never use `waitingFor` for Nathan.
